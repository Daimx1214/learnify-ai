Ohm's law describes how current, voltage and resistance are related in a simple circuit. If the temperature of a metal wire stays constant, the current flowing through it is directly proportional to the voltage across it. This relationship is written as V = I × R, where V is voltage in volts, I is current in amperes and R is resistance in ohms. For example, a 12 V battery connected across a 4 ohm resistor drives a current of 3 A. Doubling the resistance while keeping the voltage the same halves the current.
