Pythagoras' theorem describes a right-angled triangle. In a right-angled triangle, the square of the hypotenuse is equal to the sum of the squares of the other two sides. The rule is written as a² + b² = c², where c is the hypotenuse. For a triangle with sides of 3 cm and 4 cm, the hypotenuse is 5 cm, because 3² + 4² = 9 + 16 = 25, and the square root of 25 is 5. Builders use this rule to check that a corner is exactly 90 degrees.
